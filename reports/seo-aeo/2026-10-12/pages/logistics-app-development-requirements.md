### Start with one operational workflow

A logistics application can mean a driver app, truck booking platform, operations dashboard, fleet system or field-agent CRM. Those products share some building blocks, but they solve different problems. Describe one workflow from start to finish: who creates the job, who accepts it, who changes its status and who needs to see the result.

I have worked on TruckMitr and TMConnect and offer freelance logistics development for Indian and international businesses. This checklist covers decisions to make for a new project; it does not claim that every feature below was included in those earlier applications.

### Define drivers, vehicles, jobs and permissions

Who can register a driver or vehicle, and who reviews the record?
Who creates, assigns, accepts, cancels or closes a job?
Which information belongs to a driver, agent, customer or operations manager?
Which changes need a review step or activity history?
Do different branches or business customers need separate views?

Write down the allowed status changes and the person responsible for each one. This helps prevent a driver app and an office dashboard from showing inconsistent operational states. It also makes testing concrete: each role should see and change only what the workflow permits.

### Plan explicitly for weak networks

Decide which information a driver needs while offline, which actions can be queued and which actions require an immediate server response. A stored form and live vehicle location behave differently when connectivity disappears. The screen should make pending synchronisation visible rather than reporting success too early.

Define the records available offline and their retention period.
Agree on retry rules and protection against duplicate submissions.
Decide what happens if two people update the same job.
Provide clear pending, failed and completed states.
Test interrupted requests and app restarts during synchronisation.

### Scope location tracking and alerts

Describe why location is needed and who should see it. Agree on update frequency, battery expectations, foreground and background behaviour, and what the dashboard shows when location is stale. Location permissions and device limitations need to be part of the product design.

For notifications, define the event, recipient and useful next action. An assignment alert should lead to the correct job. A missed update should have a clear follow-up path. Choose provider integrations after the notification requirements are understood.

### Identify integrations and office reporting

Existing CRM or ERP and the API access it provides.
Maps, messages, payment or accounting providers.
Required job, customer and driver exports.
Reports that operations managers need daily.
Who owns the external accounts and pays recurring charges.

Avoid promising an integration before checking the available documentation, access and business rules. If you have existing data, list what must be migrated and which records must remain accessible. The first phase can focus on the most useful workflow while later integrations remain separately scoped.

### Ask for a scope you can test

For a first release, choose one driver or staff workflow, the corresponding office view and a clear definition of completion. Include the roles, error states and offline cases in the acceptance criteria. Review the working product at milestones rather than waiting until the final delivery.

### FAQs

**Do you build logistics apps for transport businesses?**

Yes. Gagan Shukla offers logistics apps, fleet and transport software development, with relevant experience through TruckMitr and TMConnect.

**What affects the cost of a logistics app?**

User roles, mobile platforms, offline workflows, tracking behaviour, integrations, reporting and data migration affect the scope. A quote follows a discussion of those requirements.

**Should a logistics app support offline use?**

Choose offline support around the actual tasks and connectivity conditions. Define local data, queued actions, retry rules and conflict handling; some features still require a network connection.

**Can I discuss this project without a call?**

Yes, send your project brief by email or WhatsApp. I use written questions and milestone reviews to clarify the scope.

**What should I send to get a useful quote?**

Send the users, essential workflow, required platforms, integrations and any existing code or data. Mention your launch dependencies and budget range if known.

**Are the prices and timelines fixed packages?**

No, I quote the actual scope and dependencies. The proposal separates development, third-party costs, acceptance checks and ongoing support.
