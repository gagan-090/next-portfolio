### Answer-first introduction

A useful custom software brief explains the business problem, users and one essential workflow before listing features. I need to see existing tools, integration dependencies and how you will approve the result. Use the copyable brief below to describe your website, app, SaaS or CRM project and send it by email or WhatsApp.

53 words. The implemented introduction is a readable answer block; AI citation is not guaranteed.

### Start with the business problem

When you contact me about freelance development, you do not need a technical specification. Start with the work your business needs to do. Explain who uses the product, what they do today and where the process becomes slow, confusing or expensive. A workflow is more useful than a long list of technologies.

For a website, this might be helping visitors understand your services and enquire. For a CRM, it might be keeping enquiries and follow-ups visible. For a SaaS product, it might be giving customers a repeatable tool they can subscribe to. For a bid management system, it might be keeping opportunities, documents and approvals together.

### Choose the first release

Describe the smallest version that would be useful to a real user. Separate required launch features from features that can wait. This helps me quote a defined scope and helps you evaluate whether the first release solves the original problem.

List the user roles, such as customer, employee, manager or administrator.
Write the three most important tasks each role must complete.
Identify the information each task needs and the result it should produce.
Separate Android, iOS, browser and admin dashboard requirements.
Share reference products and explain what you like about them.

### Make integrations and existing data visible

Payments, maps, email, accounting software, identity providers and external APIs can materially change the scope. Tell me which tools you already use, whether API documentation is available and who controls those accounts. Avoid sending passwords or customer data in your first enquiry.

If you are replacing an existing tool, describe the records you need to keep and how you can export them. Migration and historical reports should be scoped explicitly. An existing product may also need a code review before a reliable maintenance or feature quote is possible.

### Discuss budget, timeline and delivery

A budget range helps us decide what belongs in the first phase. If you do not have one, say so and I can discuss scope options. Mention any fixed launch date and the reason for it. I estimate after understanding the requirements rather than promising one price for every website or application.

Agree on a written feature scope and acceptance criteria.
Break delivery into milestones with demonstrable results.
Choose who reviews designs and approves completed work.
Include hosting, third-party charges and support expectations in the discussion.
Agree on code handover, documentation and account ownership.

### Working together from India or another country

I am based in Noida, India, and accept freelance enquiries from any country. Include your country or time zone and preferred written review deadlines. We can agree on a communication window, written updates and demonstrations that fit the project. Payment terms and currency are confirmed before work starts.

My portfolio includes the VRC Mobility website, a bid management system, HRMS and SaaS work, alongside mobile app projects. Use the project and service pages to identify relevant experience, then ask about the requirements that matter for your own product.
